Best Adjustable Bed Feature for Snoring: Why Pillow Tilt Beats a Basic Anti-Snore Button

By AUTHOR NAME, Sleep Product Specialist at Beverly Hills Bed · Updated PUBLISH DATE

Almost every adjustable bed now ships with an anti-snore button. Press it and the head section lifts a few degrees. That small change can help, because sleeping flat lets the tongue and soft tissue fall back toward the throat.

The problem is that one preset angle does not fit every body, pillow, or mattress. A pillow tilt adjustable bed adds a second, smaller motor that moves only the very top of the head section. You keep a gentle incline under your upper body and then fine tune the angle of your head and neck. Below we explain how each feature works, who benefits, and which of our bases include pillow tilt.

Short Answer: What Is the Best Adjustable Bed Feature for Snoring?

The best adjustable bed feature for snoring is an anti-snore preset combined with pillow tilt. The anti-snore preset raises your upper body slightly to keep the airway more open, and pillow tilt lets you adjust only the head angle so you find the exact position that quiets your snoring without bending your back. Good options with both features include the M3 Superior, Ergomotion Rio 6, Ergomotion Rio 5, and the 500 Series.

How an Anti-Snore Adjustable Bed Works

When you lie completely flat, gravity pulls the jaw, tongue, and soft palate backward. The airway narrows and the tissue vibrates as air passes, which is the sound we call snoring. Raising the upper body a small amount reduces that backward pull.

Research supports the idea. A 2017 study published in Sleep and Breathing found that raising the head of the bed by about 7.5 cm (roughly 3 inches) reduced breathing interruptions in many adults with obstructive sleep apnea. An anti-snore preset is simply a one-touch way to reach a mild incline like this.

Where the anti-snore button falls short

  • One fixed angle. The preset is set at the factory. It may be too little for one sleeper and too much for another.
  • It moves the whole back section. To raise your head more, you have to raise your entire torso, which some people find less comfortable for a full night.
  • Pillows shift. Many people stack pillows to get extra head lift. Stacks collapse during the night and push the chin toward the chest.

What Is Pillow Tilt on an Adjustable Bed?

Pillow tilt (sometimes called head tilt) is a separate hinge and motor at the top of the head section. It angles only the area under your pillow, not your whole back. Think of it as an adjustable pillow built into the base.

Because it is independent, you can pair it with any back angle. Use a low anti-snore incline for your torso, then add a small amount of pillow tilt until your head and neck sit in the position that keeps you breathing quietly.

Benefits of a Pillow Tilt Adjustable Bed for Snoring

  • Fine tuned head position. Small changes in head and neck angle affect airflow. Pillow tilt lets you adjust in small steps instead of accepting one preset.
  • Less torso elevation needed. You can keep your back at a low, comfortable angle and add lift only where it matters.
  • Fewer stacked pillows. The base does the lifting, so one supportive pillow stays in place all night.
  • Better for couples. On a split base, each side can set its own anti-snore and pillow tilt angle.
  • Useful beyond sleep. The same motor angles your head forward for reading or watching TV without neck strain.

How to set up pillow tilt for snoring

  1. Press the anti-snore preset first.
  2. Add pillow tilt a few seconds at a time.
  3. Stop when your head is lifted but your chin is not pushed toward your chest. Too much tilt can narrow the airway again.
  4. Save the position to a memory button if your base offers one.
  5. Ask your partner, or use a snore tracking app, to compare a few nights.

Adjustable Bed for Snoring FAQs

Does an adjustable bed really help with snoring?

For many people, yes. Raising the upper body slightly reduces how much the tongue and soft tissue fall back toward the throat.

What is the difference between anti-snore and pillow tilt?

Anti-snore is a preset that raises the whole head section to one fixed angle. Pillow tilt is a separate motor that angles only the top of the head section, so you can fine tune your head position.

How high should I raise my bed to stop snoring?

Start with the anti-snore preset, which is a gentle incline. Then add pillow tilt in small steps and stop before your chin presses toward your chest.

Can pillow tilt help my partner sleep better too?

Yes, because quieter snoring means fewer wake-ups for the person next to you. On a split king base, each partner can also set their own angle.

Is an adjustable bed a treatment for sleep apnea?

No, an adjustable bed is a comfort product and not a medical treatment. Loud snoring with gasping or daytime sleepiness can be a sign of sleep apnea, so talk with your doctor.
