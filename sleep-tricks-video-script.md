## [TRICK #1 — 0:45–1:45]
### **The 4-7-8 Breathing Method**

*[Graphic overlay: "4-7-8 Breathing"]*

This one was popularized by Dr. Andrew Weil, and it works by activating your parasympathetic nervous system — that's your body's "rest and digest" mode.

Here's how to do it:

- **Inhale** through your nose for **4 seconds**
- **Hold** your breath for **7 seconds**
- **Exhale** completely through your mouth for **8 seconds**

Repeat that cycle **4 times**.

The extended exhale is the secret sauce. It slows your heart rate and signals to your nervous system that you are safe, calm, and ready to rest. Try it right now — seriously, pause the video and try it.

## [TRICK #2 — 1:45–2:45]
### **Lower Your Room Temperature**

*[B-roll: thermostat being adjusted, cozy blanket]*

Your body needs to **drop its core temperature by about 1 to 2 degrees Fahrenheit** to initiate sleep. That's not a preference — it's biology.

The sweet spot for most people is somewhere between **65 and 68 degrees Fahrenheit** (that's 18 to 20 Celsius). If your room is too warm, your body can't complete that cooling process and you'll toss and turn all night.

If you can't control your thermostat, try:

- A **cooling mattress pad**
- Sleeping with **breathable, moisture-wicking sheets** (bamboo or linen are great)
- Keeping a **fan** pointed toward you

A cooler room = a faster trip to dreamland.

## [TRICK #3 — 2:45–3:45]
### **The Military Sleep Method**

*[Graphic: soldier silhouette, simple animation]*

This technique was reportedly developed by the U.S. military to help soldiers fall asleep in **under 2 minutes** — even in stressful environments.

Here's the process:

1. **Relax your face** — every muscle, including your jaw, tongue, and the muscles around your eyes.
2. **Drop your shoulders** as low as they'll go. Let your arms go limp, one side at a time.
3. **Exhale and relax your chest.**
4. **Relax your legs** — thighs, calves, feet.
5. **Clear your mind for 10 seconds.** Picture one of these: floating in a canoe on a calm lake, lying in a black velvet hammock in a dark room, or simply repeating "don't think" over and over.

It takes practice — about **6 weeks of consistent use** — but people who use this method swear by it.

## [TRICK #4 — 3:45–4:30]
### **Stop Looking at the Clock**

*[Close-up of phone clock, then phone being placed face-down]*

This one sounds too simple, but it's a game changer.

When you check the time in the middle of the night, your brain immediately starts calculating — "If I fall asleep right now, I'll get 4 hours and 23 minutes." That's **anxiety math**, and it will keep you wide awake.

**Turn your clock away from you.** Put your phone face-down, across the room, or in a drawer. Remove the time variable entirely.

Your alarm will still go off. I promise.

## [TRICK #5 — 4:30–5:30]
### **Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)**

*[Soft graphic animation of body scan]*

Progressive Muscle Relaxation is a technique where you **tense and then release** each muscle group in your body, working from your feet up to your face.

Here's a quick version:

- **Feet:** Curl your toes tight, hold 5 seconds, release.
- **Calves:** Flex them hard, hold, release.
- **Thighs, stomach, hands, arms, shoulders, face** — work your way up.

Each time you release, notice the wave of relaxation that follows. By the time you reach your face, most people are already drifting off.

PMR is widely used in cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) and has strong clinical support behind it.

## [TRICK #6 — 5:30–6:15]
### **No Screens 60 Minutes Before Bed**

*[Phone, TV, laptop — each with an X over it]*

I know. You've heard this before. But let me give you the "why" so it actually sticks.

Blue light from screens **suppresses melatonin production** — melatonin being the hormone that tells your brain it's nighttime and time to sleep. When you're scrolling until 11:58pm and then expecting to fall asleep at midnight, your brain is still chemically convinced it's 3 in the afternoon.

**One hour before bed: screens off.** Replace it with:

- Reading a physical book
- Journaling
- Light stretching or yoga
- A warm bath or shower

Speaking of which…

## [TRICK #7 — 6:15–7:00]
### **Take a Warm Shower or Bath 1–2 Hours Before Bed**

*[B-roll: steam, warm water]*

Here's the counterintuitive science: a **warm shower actually cools you down** for sleep.

When you step out of hot water, your body rapidly loses heat — and that drop in body temperature mimics the natural cooling your body does when it prepares for sleep. Studies show that a warm bath taken **1 to 2 hours before bed** can help you fall asleep up to **10 minutes faster** and improve overall sleep quality.

It also doubles as a wind-down ritual, which signals to your brain that sleep is coming.

## [TRICK #8 — 7:00–7:55]
### **Keep a Consistent Sleep Schedule — Even on Weekends**

*[Calendar graphic, alarm clock]*

Your body runs on a **circadian rhythm** — an internal 24-hour clock. When you go to bed and wake up at the same time every day, your body starts releasing sleep hormones right on schedule.

When you "sleep in" on weekends, you're essentially giving yourself **social jet lag** — the same disorientation as flying two time zones east. Monday night becomes brutal, and the cycle repeats.

The fix: pick a wake time and stick to it **7 days a week**. Your sleep onset — how fast you fall asleep — will improve dramatically within 1 to 2 weeks.

## [TRICK #9 — 7:55–8:55]
### **The Cognitive Shuffle Technique**

*[Fun graphic: random images — a duck, a bicycle, a cloud]*

This one is newer and a little unusual — but it's brilliant.

Developed by cognitive scientist **Dr. Luc Beaulieu-Prévost**, the Cognitive Shuffle works by giving your brain **random, unconnected images** to think about, which mimics the kind of fragmented thinking your brain naturally does as it drifts into sleep.

Here's how:

1. Pick a random word — like "BEDTIME."
2. Visualize something that starts with B — a **bear**, maybe.
3. Then E — an **elephant**.
4. Then D — a **duck**.

Keep going, making each image vivid and a little silly. Your brain can't maintain the logical, anxious thoughts that keep you awake when it's busy picturing a duck wearing a top hat.

## [TRICK #10 — 8:55–9:50]
### **Invest in the Right Sleep Environment**

*[B-roll: calm, beautiful bedroom — perhaps a Beverly Hills Bed setup]*

All of the tricks above work better when your sleep environment is dialed in. Think of it as your **sleep foundation**.

Here's a quick checklist:

- **Darkness:** Use blackout curtains or a sleep mask. Even small amounts of light disrupt melatonin.
- **Quiet:** Use a white noise machine or earplugs if your environment is noisy.
- **Scent:** Lavender aromatherapy has been shown in multiple studies to reduce anxiety and improve sleep quality.
- **Your mattress and pillow:** If you wake up with aches, or you're overheating at night, your bed might be working against you. A quality mattress that supports your body and regulates temperature is one of the best long-term investments you can make in your health.

You spend a third of your life in bed. Make sure that third is actually restoring you.
