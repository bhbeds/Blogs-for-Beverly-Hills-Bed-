The Contenders

All three mattresses share the same foundation: dual independent air chambers, one per sleep side, that let each person adjust their own firmness across 50 settings. That core technology — the ability to dial in your exact comfort rather than compromise with a partner — is what separates adjustable air mattresses from every other category. Beyond that shared premise, the three products diverge considerably.

Beverly Hills Bed 14" Air Adjustable Mattress

BHB's flagship standalone mattress arrives at 14 inches with a reversible super pillow top and internal dual air chambers — meaning no external pumps or hoses on the floor. Each side of the bed operates independently with 50 granular firmness settings. At $2,450 for a queen, it occupies the premium air mattress tier without crossing into luxury pricing. It compares directly to the Sleep Number i10 in feature set, at a significantly lower price point.

Beverly Hills Bed 14" Air Mattress + Adjustable Massaging Base (Bundle)

The complete Beverly Hills Bed sleep system. The same 14-inch mattress is paired with a full adjustable bed base featuring Bluetooth connectivity, independent head and foot articulation, zero-gravity preset positioning, and programmable massage with 10-, 20-, and 30-minute timer options — all controlled through dual wireless remotes. This bundle is BHB's most comprehensive offering: a luxury adjustable sleep ecosystem at a fraction of what comparable complete systems cost elsewhere.

Airpedic 1200

Airpedic's top-tier model, the 1200 is built around five comfort layers totaling 7.25 inches above the air chambers. The quilted pillow top incorporates 0.8 inches of ECO natural latex — a premium, biodegradable material known for its durability, breathability, and buoyant support. Total mattress height comes to 14.5 inches. The Airpedic 1200 also features the brand's proprietary Airflow Transfer System (ATS), a passive ventilation architecture designed to move body heat away from the sleep surface. It is a genuinely well-engineered product, and its $7,749 queen price reflects that — but it is a mattress only. An adjustable base is sold separately.

Price: The Elephant in the Room

The Airpedic 1200 costs $7,749 for a queen. Beverly Hills Bed's 14-inch air adjustable mattress costs $2,450 for the same size — a difference of $5,299. That's not rounding-error territory. It's a substantive financial decision, and anyone evaluating these products deserves to sit with that number before moving on to comparisons of fabric layers and cooling systems.

The gap becomes even more pronounced when you factor in the adjustable base. The Airpedic 1200 is a mattress only. If you want the full adjustable sleep experience — head articulation, foot elevation, zero-gravity positioning — you need to add a compatible base. Quality adjustable bases with massage and programmable positions run $1,000 to $2,500 or more. That brings the Airpedic complete system cost to roughly $9,000 to $10,000+.

Beverly Hills Bed's bundle includes all of that — adjustable massaging base, Bluetooth controls, zero gravity, programmable massage timers, and the 14-inch air mattress — in a single purchase at a fraction of that combined cost.

Comfort & Construction: Where the Brands Diverge

The air chambers at the core of both mattresses serve the same function: they let you set your exact firmness rather than accepting whatever a manufacturer decided was "comfortable." That shared technology — 50 settings, dual independent chambers — is what makes both products genuinely useful for couples with different sleep preferences. But the comfort layers sitting above those chambers tell a different story.

Beverly Hills Bed: The Reversible Pillow Top

BHB's 14-inch mattress features a reversible super pillow top — a practical design that extends the mattress's useful lifespan in a meaningful way. Flipping the top layer changes the feel of the sleep surface without altering the air chamber system beneath it, effectively giving you two feel profiles within one mattress. Air chambers and pumps are fully contained inside the mattress structure, so there are no external components, hoses, or pump noise on the floor.

Airpedic 1200: Five-Layer Luxury Construction

The Airpedic 1200 invests heavily in its comfort stack. Five layers totaling 7.25 inches sit above the air chambers, led by a quilted pillow top containing 0.8 inches of ECO natural latex. Natural latex is a premium material — responsive, naturally breathable, hypoallergenic, and demonstrably more durable over time than most synthetic foam alternatives. The feel is distinctive: neither the sinking sensation of memory foam nor the springy bounce of coils, but something buoyant and pressure-relieving simultaneously.

The proprietary Airflow Transfer System (ATS) adds another dimension. Rather than relying on gel infusions or perforated layers — common in competing designs — the ATS channels air actively through the mattress structure to manage heat. For sleepers who run warm, this is a meaningful differentiator that gel foams often fail to match over time.

The Honest Assessment

On pure materials engineering, the Airpedic 1200's comfort stack is more sophisticated. Natural latex is a premium material, and the ATS cooling system outperforms standard approaches. If advanced materials are your primary buying criterion and budget is secondary, that distinction is real and worth acknowledging. That said: the air technology that actually creates your customized firmness — the feature you'll interact with every single night — is functionally equivalent in both products. Both give you 50 settings and dual independent chambers. The $5,299 gap buys you a more elaborate comfort layer, not a fundamentally different sleep experience.

The Base Factor: The Value Equation Shifts Further

Here is where Beverly Hills Bed's value case becomes overwhelming. The Airpedic 1200 is a mattress — full stop. To unlock the adjustable sleep system experience (head elevation, foot elevation, zero-gravity positioning, massage), you need to source and purchase a compatible adjustable base separately.

Beverly Hills Bed's bundle delivers the 14-inch air mattress fully integrated with an adjustable massaging base: Bluetooth connectivity, programmable massage with three timer durations, zero-gravity preset, and dual wireless remotes — all in one purchase at a fraction of the Airpedic-plus-base combined cost. For most buyers, the bundle represents not just the better value, but the more complete and convenient path to a luxury sleep system.

Warranty and Protection

A mattress at this price point is a long-term investment. Warranty terms matter.

Beverly Hills Bed backs both of its 14-inch air mattresses with a 25-year prorated warranty: the first 3 years are full coverage, followed by 65–75% coverage for the remaining 22 years. For a product with internal air chambers and mechanisms, that's substantial protection.

Airpedic's 1200 carries a 20-year limited warranty, with full coverage for the first 2 years and prorated coverage thereafter. The air pump is covered separately for 10 years (full for 2, prorated for 8). Airpedic also includes a 120-night sleep trial — a genuine advantage BHB does not currently match. If you need a risk-free audition period before committing, that trial matters.

The Bottom Line

The Airpedic 1200 is a legitimately excellent mattress. Its natural latex comfort layers and Airflow Transfer System represent real engineering investments, and the 120-night trial offers peace of mind that Beverly Hills Bed does not currently match. If materials quality is your primary criterion and price is secondary, it delivers.

But for the vast majority of buyers, the math is difficult to argue with. Beverly Hills Bed's 14-inch air adjustable mattress delivers the same core sleep technology — 50 firmness settings, dual independent chambers, wireless control — for $2,450, saving over $5,000 versus the Airpedic queen. And if you want a complete, fully adjustable sleep system with massage, zero gravity, and Bluetooth controls, the BHB bundle packages all of that at a price that remains a fraction of what the Airpedic 1200 costs before a base is even added.

The question isn't whether the Airpedic 1200 is good — it is. The question is whether its specific advantages over the BHB lineup justify spending two to four times as much. For most buyers, they don't.
