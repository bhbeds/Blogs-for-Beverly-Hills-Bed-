What Is a Hi-Low Adjustable Bed?

A hi-low adjustable bed raises and lowers the entire sleep surface, not just the head and foot. Lowering it makes getting in and out of bed easier and reduces the distance of a fall. Raising it brings the mattress up to a comfortable working height so a caregiver can help with repositioning, dressing, or changing linens without bending over.

Standard adjustable bases only incline. Many basic home hospital beds lift but use crank or semi-electric controls. A fully electric hospital bed for home use moves every function with a motor, and that is the category the Luxurious Model sits in.

Key Features of This Hospital Bed for Home Use

Vertical lift and forward tilt

The base rises straight up 10 inches, so the bed can sit low for sleeping and high for transfers or caregiving. The 10 degree forward tilt angles the mattress toward the edge, which helps a person move from lying to sitting to standing with less effort.

Voice command and wireless remote

Say "Hello Base" followed by a command to adjust the bed hands-free through the built-in microphone. No app or smartphone is needed. The wireless remote does the same job for users or caregivers who prefer buttons, and it includes Flat, Zero Gravity, and 2 programmable memory positions.

Head and foot incline with Zero Gravity

The head and foot move independently. Zero Gravity raises the legs slightly above the heart to take pressure off the lower back, a position many people use for reading, watching TV, or resting after a long day.

Massage, night light, and USB charging

Head and foot massage runs at three intensities with a 10, 20, or 30 minute timer. An under-bed night light helps with nighttime bathroom trips, and USB ports keep a phone or medical alert device charged within reach.

Who should choose a different bed

If a doctor has prescribed a clinical hospital bed with specific features such as Trendelenburg positioning, full-length side rails, or a pressure-redistribution mattress system for wound care, follow that prescription. If you only want head and foot incline and do not need lift, a standard adjustable base costs less.
