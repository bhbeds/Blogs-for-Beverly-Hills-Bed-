RV King Size vs RV Short King Dimensions: The Complete Comparison

Shopping for an RV king mattress sounds simple until you discover that "king" doesn't mean the same thing in a motorhome as it does in a bedroom. The difference between an RV king and an RV short king is only five inches — but those five inches determine whether your mattress fits your slide-out, your floor plan, and your budget. Here's everything you need to know before you buy.

RV manufacturers build sleeping quarters around the constraints of road-legal vehicle widths and the mechanical reality of slide-out rooms. That means the mattresses that go inside them often don't match standard residential sizes. Two of the most commonly confused RV mattress sizes are the RV king (72″ × 80″) and the RV short king (72″ × 75″) — and getting them mixed up is an expensive mistake.

This guide breaks down the exact RV king mattress dimensions for both sizes, compares them against each other and against a standard residential king, shows you how to measure your RV sleeping area correctly, and helps you decide which size — and which mattress type — is the right fit for your rig.

What Is an RV King Mattress? (72″ × 80″)

RV King — 72″ Wide × 80″ Long

Width: 72 inches (6 ft) Length: 80 inches (6 ft 8 in) Also called: RV King 72×80

The RV king mattress measures 72 inches wide by 80 inches long — giving it the same length as a standard residential king or queen, but four inches narrower in width. This is the larger of the two RV king variants, and it's the size found most often in Class A motorhomes and large fifth-wheel trailers with spacious master bedroom slide-outs.

At 80 inches long, the RV king comfortably accommodates sleepers up to about 6 feet 4 inches without feeling cramped. The full-length dimension makes it the preferred choice for taller couples who spend extended periods on the road and want a sleep experience that closely mirrors sleeping at home.

Because the RV king shares its length with standard mattresses, some RV owners mistakenly try to fit a residential king (76″ × 80″) into their slide-out — only to discover those extra four inches in width make it impossible to close the slide. Always measure your sleeping area before purchasing.

Quick Tip

The RV king size is sometimes listed as the "72×80" or simply "king RV mattress" by manufacturers. If your platform is labeled king but measures 72 inches across rather than 76, you need an RV king — not a residential king.

What Is an RV Short King Mattress? (72″ × 75″)

RV Short King — 72″ Wide × 75″ Long

Width: 72 inches (6 ft) Length: 75 inches (6 ft 3 in) Also called: Short King RV / RV King Short

The RV short king mattress measures 72 inches wide by 75 inches long — identical in width to the standard RV king, but five inches shorter from head to foot. This size is the most widely produced RV king format and fits the majority of Class C motorhomes, mid-size fifth wheels, and travel trailers with bedroom slide-outs designed for a "short king" platform.

The five-inch reduction in length exists purely for practical reasons: RV bedroom compartments frequently sit directly above wheel wells, storage bays, or mechanical components that physically shorten the available floor space. Those five inches can make the difference between a slide that closes cleanly and one that doesn't close at all.

For most average-height sleepers — roughly 5 feet 10 inches and under — the RV short king provides more than adequate legroom. Taller sleepers sometimes use a pillow tuck or a different sleeping angle on wider platforms, but generally find 75 inches sufficient for a comfortable night's sleep. The RV short king 72×75 has become the de facto "standard king" of the RV world, simply because it fits more rigs.

Quick Tip

RV short king mattresses are also sold as "short queen" in some categories — but the dimensions are different (60″×75″ for short queen vs. 72″×75″ for short king). Always confirm width before ordering. The RV short king is 12 inches wider than the short queen.

RV King Mattress Size vs Regular King: What's the Difference?

One of the most common questions from first-time RV buyers is whether a standard residential king mattress will fit in their RV's master bedroom. The short answer is: almost never. Here's why.

A standard king mattress measures 76 inches wide by 80 inches long. An RV king measures 72 inches wide by 80 inches long. Those four inches of missing width matter enormously in a slide-out room, where the wall clearance is machined to exact tolerances. Forcing a 76-inch mattress into a 72-inch opening will either prevent the slide from operating or create constant friction that damages both the mattress and the slide mechanism over time.

The inverse is equally true: placing a 72-inch RV king mattress in a residential bed frame designed for a 76-inch standard king will leave visible, uncomfortable gaps on both sides. RV king mattresses are a purpose-built size — and that's exactly why quality options matter. A cheap, poorly constructed mattress in a non-standard size is nearly impossible to replace locally on the road.

RV King or RV Short King: How to Know Which Size You Need

The only reliable way to determine your correct RV mattress size is to measure the sleeping platform directly — not the old mattress, and not the spec sheet. RV interiors are not always built to exact published dimensions, and wear over time can shift tolerances. Follow these steps:

  • Remove the existing mattress completely from the platform.
  • Measure the platform width at the widest point (typically the head of the bed).
  • Measure the platform length from head wall to foot wall or the point where the slide closes.
  • Subtract ½ inch from each dimension to allow for clearance.
  • Match your measurements against the RV king (72″×80″) or RV short king (72″×75″) standard.

If your platform measures approximately 71.5″×79.5″, order an RV king. If it measures approximately 71.5″×74.5″, order an RV short king. Platforms that fall between these two lengths — roughly 75″ to 79″ — may be custom dimensions, and in that case a custom-cut mattress is the right solution.

What to Look for in the Best RV King Mattress

Beyond getting the RV king mattress dimensions right, several factors separate a mattress that holds up over years of RV use from one that deteriorates after a single season. Here's what matters most:

Firmness Adjustability

RV travel means sleeping in different climates, altitudes, and temperature ranges — all of which affect how a foam mattress feels and how much it compresses. An air adjustable mattress eliminates this variability entirely. You dial in your preferred feel regardless of whether you're parked in a humid Florida campsite in August or a cold Rocky Mountain campground in October. This is the single biggest advantage air mattresses have over memory foam for RV use.

Weight and Profile

Standard residential mattresses can weigh 80 to 130 pounds. That's weight your RV is carrying on every trip. RV-specific mattresses are typically constructed to deliver comfort at lower weights — important both for fuel efficiency and for keeping your rig within its GVWR (gross vehicle weight rating). A 9.5-inch profile also fits the majority of RV platforms without requiring a custom base adjustment.

Edge Support

In an RV bedroom, you're often getting in and out of bed in a confined space, which means sitting on the mattress edge frequently. Poor edge support leads to premature sagging and an uncomfortable seating surface. Look for reinforced perimeter construction in any RV king mattress you consider.

Temperature Regulation

RV sleeping compartments heat up faster than home bedrooms and often lack the airflow that residential rooms provide. A mattress with breathable cover materials and a non-heat-trapping comfort layer — like the latex or gel-infused options — makes a meaningful difference in sleep quality during warm-weather travel.

RV King Mattress Toppers: A Note on Sizing

If you're upgrading the comfort of an existing RV king without replacing the whole mattress, a mattress topper is a cost-effective option. However, the same sizing rules apply: you need a topper cut to 72″×80″ for the RV king or 72″×75″ for the RV short king. Standard king toppers (76″×80″) will overhang the platform and bunch up against the slide walls.

Some RV owners purchase a California king topper (72″×84″) and trim it to their needed length, since California king shares the same 72-inch width as both RV king sizes. This is a viable approach if you're comfortable cutting foam. For a topper designed and cut specifically for RV dimensions, look for brands that specialize in RV bedding — or consider whether a full mattress replacement better serves your needs long-term.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1: What are the exact RV king mattress dimensions?

The standard RV king mattress measures 72 inches wide by 80 inches long (sometimes written as 72×80). This is four inches narrower than a residential standard king (76″×80″) but shares the same length. The RV short king variant measures 72″×75″ — the same width but five inches shorter. Always measure your RV sleeping platform directly before ordering, as manufacturer tolerances can vary.

Q2: What is the difference between an RV king and a regular king mattress?

A regular residential king mattress is 76 inches wide by 80 inches long. An RV king is 72 inches wide by 80 inches long — four inches narrower. That width reduction is not cosmetic: it exists because RV slide-out rooms are built to tighter tolerances than home bedrooms. A standard residential king will not fit in most RV king platforms and may prevent the slide-out from operating properly. The length (80″) is identical between both sizes.

Q3: Is an RV short king the same as an RV king?

No. An RV short king (72″×75″) and an RV king (72″×80″) share the same width but differ by five inches in length. The short king is the more common size, fitting the majority of Class C motorhomes and mid-size fifth wheels. The full RV king is found primarily in Class A motorhomes and larger fifth-wheel floor plans with extended master bedroom slide-outs. Ordering the wrong length means the mattress will either be too short for the platform or too long to allow the slide to close.

Q4: Can I use a California king mattress in my RV instead of an RV king?

A California king mattress measures 72 inches wide by 84 inches long — the same width as both RV king sizes, but four inches longer than an RV king and nine inches longer than an RV short king. In most cases, a California king will be too long for the platform, preventing the slide from closing properly. However, some RV owners have successfully trimmed a California king foam mattress to the correct RV king length. This is only practical with an all-foam mattress and requires precise cutting. A purpose-built RV king mattress is a safer and more reliable choice.

Q5: What sheets fit an RV king or RV short king mattress?

Standard residential king sheets (designed for a 76″×80″ mattress) will fit loosely on an RV king (72″×80″) because the length matches but the width is four inches narrower. They won't stay tucked as neatly and may bunch. For an RV short king (72″×75″), residential king sheets will be both too wide and too long. You'll need sheets specifically sized for your RV mattress dimensions — many RV bedding specialists sell sheets cut to 72×80 and 72×75 to ensure a proper, wrinkle-free fit.

Q6: Why is an air adjustable mattress a good choice for an RV king bed?

An air adjustable mattress is particularly well-suited to RV use for three reasons. First, RV sleeping temperatures fluctuate significantly between climates — air chambers maintain their firmness regardless of temperature, while memory foam softens in heat and stiffens in cold. Second, RV couples often have different firmness preferences, and dual-chamber air adjustable mattresses let each sleeper set their own side independently. Third, RV platforms see constant vibration from road travel; air mattresses distribute that stress more evenly than coil or foam constructions, extending the mattress's lifespan considerably.
