What Is an Incline Bed? Incline Bed Frames & Adjustable Incline Beds Compared

Beverly Hills Bed  |  September 4, 2026  |  7 min read

If you've been told to "sleep on an incline" for reflux, swelling, breathing, or recovery, you've probably run into two very different products both called an incline bed. One is a fixed frame that props the mattress up at a set angle. The other is a motorized adjustable incline bed that changes position at the touch of a button. Here's what an incline bed actually is, how the options compare, and which type fits your situation.

Short Answer

An incline bed is any bed set up so the head, foot, or both ends sit higher than flat. A fixed incline bed frame uses risers, wedges, or a built-in slope to hold one angle. An adjustable incline bed uses a motorized base to raise and lower the head and foot to any angle, usually 0-60 degrees at the head and 0-45 degrees at the foot. Both are used to ease acid reflux, snoring, swelling, and back pain, but an adjustable incline bed frame gives you far more control than a fixed one.

What Is an Incline Bed?

An incline bed is simply a bed that doesn't sit flat. Instead of a level sleep surface, the head section, foot section, or both are raised above horizontal. Elevating the upper body helps gravity work with you instead of against you: it keeps stomach acid down, opens the airway, and takes pressure off the lower back. Elevating the legs and feet helps blood and fluid drain back toward the heart instead of pooling in the ankles.

There are two main ways to get a bed incline, and the difference matters more than most shoppers expect.

Incline Bed Frame (Fixed Incline)

A traditional incline bed frame holds one set angle. This can be a wedge-shaped frame, a set of bed risers under the headboard legs, or a frame built with a permanent slope. It's inexpensive and needs no power, but the angle is locked in. You can't flatten it out to sleep normally or adjust it foot-by-foot for a different symptom.

Adjustable Incline Bed (Motorized Incline)

An adjustable incline bed, sometimes called an adjustable incline bed frame or a hi-low adjustable bed, uses an electric base with independent head and foot motors. You choose the exact incline with a remote or app, save favorite positions, and return the bed to flat whenever you like. This is the type used in home care, senior care, and most hospital bed incline setups today.

Health Benefits of Sleeping on an Incline

Acid Reflux and Heartburn Relief

Raising the head of the bed 15-20 degrees uses gravity to keep stomach acid where it belongs. This is one of the most common reasons people search for an adjustable incline bed frame instead of stacking pillows, which flatten out during the night.

Snoring and Breathing Support

A head incline around 30 degrees keeps the airway more open by preventing the tongue and soft palate from collapsing backward. Many people combine this with a side-sleeping position for even better results.

Swelling and Circulation

Elevating the foot section reduces pooling in the legs and ankles and encourages fluid to drain back toward the heart. This is a common use case for a hospital bed incline in recovery and home care settings.

Back and Neck Pain

A slight bend at the head and knees, sometimes called a zero gravity position, takes pressure off the lumbar spine by distributing weight more evenly than lying flat.

Who Actually Needs a Bed with Incline?

  • Seniors and home care patients who need help getting in and out of bed, plus positioning support for circulation and pressure relief.
  • Post-surgery and recovery patients who need to keep swelling down or avoid lying fully flat.
  • People with reflux, sleep apnea, or chronic snoring who benefit from a consistent, adjustable head incline.
  • Couples with different needs who want a split king adjustable incline bed so each side moves independently.

How to Choose an Adjustable Incline Bed Frame

Head and Foot Range

Look for independent head and foot motors so you're not locked into one incline. A wider range gives you more positions to try as your needs change.

Hi-Low Height

A hi-low adjustable bed can also raise or lower the entire bed height, which makes it far easier for caregivers to assist and for users to transfer in and out safely.

Extra Features

Massage, voice control, USB charging, and programmable memory positions are common on today's adjustable incline beds and make daily use noticeably easier for seniors and caregivers alike.

Size and Fit

Twin XL, queen, king, and split king are the most common sizes for an adjustable incline bed frame. Split king lets two sleepers each set their own incline on a shared bed.

Incline Bed FAQs

What is an incline bed used for?

An incline bed is used to raise the head, foot, or both above flat, which helps with acid reflux, snoring, swelling, and back pain. It's used at home, in senior care, and in hospitals for recovery and comfort.

What's the difference between an incline bed frame and an adjustable incline bed?

An incline bed frame is usually fixed at one angle, using wedges or a sloped design. An adjustable incline bed uses electric motors so you can change the head and foot angle whenever you want.

What angle should an incline bed be for acid reflux?

Most people find relief with the head raised around 15 to 20 degrees. An adjustable incline bed frame lets you fine-tune this instead of guessing with stacked pillows.

Are hospital bed incline features available for home use?

Yes, many adjustable incline beds now include the same hi-low height and head or foot incline features found on hospital beds. This makes home care and recovery much easier without needing a clinical setting.

Can I put an adjustable incline base on any bed frame?

Most adjustable incline bases fit standard bed frames or can be used freestanding with legs. Always check the base's size and clearance against your existing frame or headboard before ordering.
