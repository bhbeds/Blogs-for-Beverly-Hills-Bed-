Frequently Asked Questions

Why are Beverly Hills Bed's pumps inside the mattress — and why does it matter?

Most air beds house their pumps and hoses externally, either tucked under the bed frame or on the floor nearby. Over time, those exposed components attract dust, can be accidentally kicked or snagged, and are more prone to wear. Beverly Hills Bed fully encases both air pumps inside the mattress structure, so there's nothing on the floor to worry about. It also means the system is quieter and the mechanical parts are protected by the same materials that protect the rest of the mattress.

How does the flippable comfort layer on the Beverly Hills Bed work?

The 14" Beverly Hills Bed has a unique layered construction: beneath the pillow top and above the air bladders sits a flippable comfort layer. This means you can flip that layer to change the overall feel of the mattress — giving you a different surface texture and pressure response — entirely independently of your air firmness setting. It's an added dimension of adjustability that most competitors simply don't offer.

Does Beverly Hills Bed have an app? Can I still control the firmness easily?

Beverly Hills Bed currently uses a wireless remote rather than a smartphone app. The remote lets you adjust firmness in single increments across a 1–50 scale, so the precision is excellent — you just won't be able to control it from your phone. Several competitors (iSense, Personal Comfort, Night Air Beds, Saatva, Airpedic) offer app integration if smartphone control is a must-have for you. An app update may be on the Beverly Hills Bed roadmap, so check beverlyhillsbed.com for the latest.

What's included in the Beverly Hills Bed split king adjustable base bundle?

The $4,880 split king bundle includes the pair of 14" air adjustable mattresses plus a massaging adjustable base with lumbar support, pillow tilt, wall-hugger technology, and app control — all the premium base features. That's a fully loaded package at a price that's $2,223–$8,959 less than every competitor's comparable bundle.

How do air adjustable beds compare to traditional innerspring or memory foam mattresses?

Traditional mattresses have a fixed firmness — what you feel on day one is what you're stuck with (or you buy a topper). Air adjustable beds let each sleeper set their own firmness independently, which is a major advantage for couples with different sleep preferences. They're also easier to adjust as your preferences change over time — pregnancy, injury recovery, weight changes — without replacing the mattress. The tradeoff is higher upfront cost and slightly more mechanical complexity, though modern air beds like Beverly Hills Bed's design are engineered to minimize that complexity.

The Beverly Hills Bed 14" split king mattress at $3,450 is the lowest-priced option on this list, and the fully loaded base bundle at $4,880 beats every competitor by at least $2,200. It's also the only brand on this list with in-mattress pumps and a flippable comfort layer — features that typically cost more elsewhere. The one area where competitors pull ahead is app connectivity and, in some cases, warranty length.
