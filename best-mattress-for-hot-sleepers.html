Best Mattress for Hot Sleepers: The Complete Cooling Guide

Beverly Hills Bed  |  August 27, 2026  |  6 min read

Waking up drenched in sweat, kicking off the blankets, or flipping the pillow to the cool side all night are signs your mattress is working against you, not for you. Hot sleepers do not just need a thinner blanket. They need a mattress built with breathable, temperature neutral materials from the inside out. Traditional memory foam is the biggest culprit: its dense, closed cell structure absorbs body heat and holds it against your skin for hours. This guide breaks down why you sleep hot, which mattress types actually stay cool, and how to pick the right one for your body.

Short Answer

The best mattresses for hot sleepers are natural latex mattresses and air adjustable mattresses. Latex has an open cell structure that allows continuous airflow, while air adjustable mattresses use air chambers instead of dense foam, so there is no insulating layer to trap heat. Both sleep significantly cooler than memory foam or traditional innerspring hybrids. Why Do Some People Sleep Hot at Night?

Memory Foam Traps Body Heat

Standard memory foam is dense and closed cell by design. That density is what makes it contour to your body, but it also blocks airflow almost completely. Heat has nowhere to go, so it builds up between your body and the mattress surface for hours.

Poor Airflow in the Support Core

Many mattresses use thick, uninterrupted foam layers in the support core. Without channels for air to move through, heat and moisture get trapped below the surface no matter how cool the top layer feels at first touch.

Room Temperature and Bedding Choices

Heavy comforters, synthetic sheets, and a bedroom above 68 degrees all make heat retention worse. These factors matter, but they cannot fix a mattress that is fundamentally built to hold heat rather than release it.

What Actually Makes a Mattress Sleep Cooler

Open Cell, Breathable Materials

Natural latex is naturally aerated, with an open cell structure that lets air pass through the material instead of getting trapped inside it. This constant airflow pulls heat away from your body throughout the night.

No Foam Insulation Layer at All

Air adjustable mattresses replace foam entirely with air chambers wrapped in a breathable fabric cover. Without a thick foam layer acting as insulation, there is nothing to trap heat against your skin in the first place.

Moisture Wicking Covers

A breathable, moisture wicking cover pulls sweat away from the surface so it evaporates instead of soaking in. This helps, but it works best when paired with a naturally cooling material underneath, not as a fix on its own.

The Best Mattress Types for Hot Sleepers

Natural Latex Mattresses

Latex is one of the most naturally breathable mattress materials available. Its open cell structure allows air to circulate continuously, so heat never builds up the way it does in memory foam. Latex is also naturally responsive, giving you contouring pressure relief without the slow, heat trapping sink of foam.

They are also an excellent choice for couples who run at different temperatures or want different firmness on each side.

Mattresses to Avoid If You Sleep Hot

Traditional all foam memory foam mattresses are the toughest option for hot sleepers, even with a cooling gel infusion, because the base layer still traps heat. Cooling gels only affect the very top layer and typically lose effectiveness within the first year of use.

How to Choose the Right Cooling Mattress for Your Body

Match Firmness to Your Sleep Position

Side sleepers generally do best with a medium latex feel that cushions the shoulders and hips. Back and stomach sleepers benefit from a medium firm to firm setting, which an air adjustable mattress lets you dial in exactly.

Consider Your Body Weight

Heavier sleepers compress foam faster, which increases heat retention over time as the material breaks down. Latex resists this kind of long term compression, and air chambers do not compress or soften at all.

Think About Long Term Adjustability

Firmness needs can change with age, injury, or a new sleep partner. An air adjustable mattress lets you make those changes yourself instead of buying a new mattress every time your needs shift.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best mattress for hot sleepers?

Natural latex and air adjustable mattresses are the best options because both use breathable, non foam designs that do not trap heat. Memory foam should generally be avoided if overheating at night is a concern.

Does memory foam sleep hot?

Yes, standard memory foam is dense and closed cell, which traps body heat against the skin. Cooling gel infusions help slightly at first but fade in effectiveness within a year or so.

Are air adjustable mattresses good for couples?

Yes, air adjustable mattresses let each side of the bed be set to a different firmness independently. This also makes them a great option when one partner sleeps hotter than the other.

Is a latex mattress better than memory foam for heat?

Yes, latex has a naturally open cell structure that allows continuous airflow, unlike dense memory foam. This makes latex sleep significantly cooler while still offering similar pressure relieving support.

How long do latex and air adjustable mattresses last?

Natural latex mattresses typically last 12 to 20 years, longer than most memory foam or innerspring options. Air adjustable mattresses also have a long lifespan since the air chambers do not sag or break down like foam.
