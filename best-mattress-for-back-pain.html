What Is the Best Mattress for Back Pain?

Beverly Hills Bed  |  June 2, 2026  |  8 min read

Back pain and poor sleep form a vicious cycle — pain disrupts sleep, and poor sleep lowers your pain threshold. The right mattress can break that cycle. Here's everything you need to know to find the best mattress for back pain, lower back pain, and waking up feeling restored.

Why Your Mattress Is the First Place to Look

An estimated 80% of adults will experience significant back pain at some point in their lives, and a growing body of research links mattress quality directly to spinal health. When your mattress is too soft, your hips and shoulders sink unevenly, bowing the spine out of alignment. When it's too firm, it fails to cushion pressure points at the shoulders, hips, and lumbar curve — creating painful contact zones that build up over hours.

A 2015 study published in Sleep Health found that medium-firm mattresses significantly reduced chronic low back pain, improved sleep quality, and decreased disability compared to participants' existing bedding. The spine needs support and pressure relief simultaneously — and that balance is exactly what the best mattress for back pain delivers.

If you wake up stiff and feel better after an hour of movement, your mattress is almost certainly the culprit. The good news: choosing the right mattress for lower back pain, hip pain, or neck and shoulder discomfort is more straightforward than most people think.

What Firmness Is Best for Back Pain?

The most common question we hear is: should I get a firm mattress for back pain? The short answer is no — at least not for most people. A mattress that is too firm prevents the natural spinal curves from being accommodated, which actually increases back pain rather than relieving it.

The doctor-recommended mattress for back pain almost universally lands in the medium-firm range (6–7 on a 10-point scale). This firmness level supports the lumbar curve without creating pressure points at the shoulders and hips. Research backs this up: a medium-firm mattress has been shown to be the best mattress for lower back pain across multiple sleep position types.

How Firmness Matches Your Body Weight

Firmness is felt differently depending on your weight. A 130-lb person will sink very little into a medium mattress, while a 250-lb person will compress it significantly more.

  • Under 130 lbs: Medium (5–6) typically feels correct. A firm mattress may feel too rigid and create pressure points.
  • 130–230 lbs: Medium-firm (6–7) is the sweet spot — the most widely recommended firmness for back pain at average body weight.
  • Over 230 lbs: Firm (7–8) provides better support and prevents excessive sinkage that misaligns the spine. A medium mattress may feel too soft under heavier body weight.

Memory Foam

Memory foam contours closely to the body, filling the space between the mattress and the lumbar curve. This makes it excellent at pressure relief and preventing the "hammock effect" where hips sink below the shoulders. High-density memory foam is the best mattress topper for back pain as well — it's an affordable upgrade before committing to a full mattress replacement.

The main drawback is heat retention. Look for gel-infused or open-cell memory foam if you sleep warm. Explore our mattress toppers collection for pressure-relieving options.

Hybrid Mattresses

A hybrid combines a pocketed coil base (for deep support and airflow) with comfort layers of foam or latex on top. This is the most doctor-recommended mattress for back pain because it delivers spinal support from the coils while the foam layers relieve pressure at the hips and shoulders. The coil system also prevents the excessive sinkage that can misalign the spine.

Hybrid mattresses are particularly effective for the best mattress for back and hip pain — a combination that demands both pressure relief and structural support. Browse our full mattress collection to compare hybrid options.

Beverly Hills Bed Tip

Look for a hybrid with individually wrapped (pocketed) coils rather than interconnected springs. Pocketed coils respond independently to each part of the body, providing zoned support that is ideal for lumbar pain relief.

Latex Mattresses

Natural latex offers a responsive, buoyant feel that contours without the deep sink of memory foam. It springs back immediately when you change position, which reduces the effort required to move during sleep — important for back pain sufferers who shift frequently to find relief. Latex is also naturally breathable, hypoallergenic, and among the most durable mattress materials available.

A firm latex mattress for back pain is a popular choice for heavier sleepers and strict back sleepers who need responsive, consistent support without foam's softening over time.

Can a Mattress Topper Help With Back Pain?

A mattress topper for back pain is a cost-effective solution when your existing mattress is fundamentally supportive but lacks the pressure relief layer your body needs. The best mattress topper for back pain adds 2–4 inches of memory foam or latex over your current surface, softening pressure points without sacrificing the underlying support structure.

If your mattress is too firm and creating hip and shoulder pressure, a medium-soft memory foam topper can make a significant difference. However, if your mattress is already sagging or too soft, no topper will compensate for the lost spinal support — replacement is the right call. Shop our mattress toppers for back pain or explore pillows designed to support spinal alignment.

Beverly Hills Bed Tip

Test your mattress: lie on your back and slide a flat hand under your lower back. If there's a large gap, your mattress is too firm and a soft topper will help. If your hand can barely fit, your mattress is too soft and you need more support — not a softer topper.

How an Adjustable Base Can Transform Back Pain Relief

An adjustable bed base may be the single most impactful upgrade for chronic back pain sufferers. By elevating the head and foot independently, an adjustable base allows you to achieve a zero-gravity position — where the knees are slightly elevated above the heart, the lumbar spine decompresses, and pressure on the intervertebral discs is dramatically reduced.

Zero-gravity positioning has been used in medical and therapeutic settings for years. It takes compression off the spine that gravity creates during both waking hours and flat sleeping. Many people report that sleeping in zero-gravity for the first time is the first genuinely pain-free night they've had in years.

An adjustable base also allows for fine-tuning: slightly elevating the head reduces snoring (which can disrupt restorative sleep and worsen pain perception), and raising the foot end relieves sciatic nerve pressure for those with lower back and leg pain. Seniors and Back Pain: Special Considerations

The best mattress for seniors with back pain typically features a medium-firm profile with a surface that is easy to get on and off — avoiding mattresses that are either too low or too plush to provide stable leverage. Adjustable bases are particularly valuable for older adults because they allow repositioning without getting out of bed, which reduces strain on arthritic joints and the lower back. Best Mattress for Back Pain on a Budget

Finding the best mattress for back pain on a budget doesn't mean settling for inadequate support. The key is prioritizing function over features: a straightforward medium-firm memory foam or hybrid mattress from a reputable brand will outperform an expensive innerspring when it comes to spinal alignment and pressure relief.

Consider these strategies for getting great back pain relief without overspending:

  • Start with a quality mattress topper ($80–$250) if your current mattress has life left in it.
  • Look for direct-to-consumer brands that eliminate retailer markup — the same materials at significantly lower price points.
  • Prioritize trial periods and return policies. The best mattress for back pain is one your body has tested for 30–60 nights, not just 10 minutes in a showroom.
  • A medium-firm all-foam mattress in the $400–$800 range often provides equivalent back pain relief to a premium hybrid at twice the price.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Is a firm mattress better for back pain?

Not necessarily. Research consistently shows that a medium-firm mattress (6–7 out of 10) is the best mattress for back pain for most adults. A mattress that is too firm fails to accommodate the natural curves of the spine and can create painful pressure points at the hips and shoulders — especially for side sleepers. Firm is appropriate only for heavier-weight sleepers (230+ lbs) or strict back sleepers who need extra resistance against sinkage.

Q: What type of mattress do doctors recommend for back pain?

Most orthopedic and sleep medicine specialists recommend a medium-firm hybrid or high-density memory foam mattress for back pain. The ideal mattress provides lumbar support while relieving pressure at the shoulders and hips. Many doctors also recommend pairing a supportive mattress with an adjustable base to allow zero-gravity positioning, which actively decompresses the spine during sleep.

Q: Can the wrong mattress cause back pain?

Yes, absolutely. A mattress that is too soft allows the hips to sag, creating a "banana curve" in the spine that strains the lower back overnight. A mattress that is too firm prevents the natural lumbar curve from being supported and creates pressure-point pain at the hips and shoulders. If you consistently wake up with back stiffness that improves within 30 minutes of getting up and moving, your mattress is very likely contributing to or causing your pain.

Q: How often should I replace my mattress if I have back pain?

Most mattresses should be replaced every 7–10 years. However, if you have chronic back pain, pay attention to signs of wear earlier: visible sagging or indentations deeper than 1 inch, loss of edge support, or a noticeable increase in morning stiffness are all signs the mattress has lost its ability to support spinal alignment. High-quality latex and hybrid mattresses typically outlast foam-only options by 2–3 years.

Q: Is a mattress topper enough to relieve back pain, or do I need a new mattress?

A mattress topper for back pain works well if your existing mattress is structurally sound but too firm — the topper adds the pressure-relief layer your body needs. However, if your mattress is sagging, excessively soft, or more than 8–10 years old, a topper cannot compensate for lost support. In that case, a new mattress is the more effective solution. A firm mattress topper for back pain (3-inch medium-firm memory foam) is one of the most popular and cost-effective short-term solutions.

Q: What is the best sleeping position for lower back pain?

Sleeping on your back with a pillow placed under your knees is widely considered the best position for lower back pain — it maintains the natural lumbar curve and distributes body weight evenly. Side sleeping with a firm pillow between the knees is also beneficial, as it prevents the top knee from dropping and twisting the pelvis. Stomach sleeping is the position most associated with lower back pain aggravation and should be avoided or minimized.
