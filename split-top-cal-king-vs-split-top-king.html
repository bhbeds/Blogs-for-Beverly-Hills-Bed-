Split Top Cal King Adjustable Bed vs Split Top King

Beverly Hills Bed  |  August 21, 2026  |  7 min read

A split top adjustable bed already solves the biggest problem couples run into: two people, two different sleep needs, one mattress. The next decision is size. California King and King look similar on paper, but the four inches split between them changes how the bed fits your room, your height, and your nightly routine. Below is a straight comparison of Split Top Cal King and Split Top King, plus where to shop both in Beverly Hills Bed's split adjustable lineup.

Short Answer

A Split Top Cal King measures 72 inches wide by 84 inches long, four inches longer than a Split Top King. It is the better pick for taller sleepers who need extra legroom and for narrower bedrooms. A Split Top King measures 76 inches wide by 80 inches long, four inches wider than Cal King, and is the better pick for couples who want more shoulder room or share the bed with kids or pets. Total sleeping surface is nearly identical between the two; the difference is only how that space is distributed.

Split Top Cal King vs Split Top King: The Core Difference

Both sizes use the same split top adjustable design: the base and mattress divide down the center so each sleeper raises, reclines, or firms up their own side without disturbing the other. The mattress size is the only variable. California King trades width for length. King trades length for width. Neither one is objectively bigger; they are shaped for different bodies and different rooms.

California King Dimensions and Fit

A California King measures 72 inches wide by 84 inches long. The extra four inches of length compared to a standard King makes it the go-to choice for anyone over roughly 6 feet 2 inches tall who normally has their feet hang off the end of a mattress. Its narrower 72-inch width also means a Cal King frame can fit through tighter doorways, hallways, and staircases than a King, which matters in older homes or upper-floor bedrooms.

King Dimensions and Fit

A standard King measures 76 inches wide by 80 inches long. Those extra four inches of width give each side of a split top King noticeably more shoulder and elbow room, which matters most for couples who move around at night or share the bed with a child or a pet. The tradeoff is four inches less length, so very tall sleepers may still feel the mattress is short.

How to Decide Between the Two

Choose Split Top Cal King If

You or your partner is taller than about 6 feet 2 inches, your bedroom or hallway is on the narrow side, or you simply want the extra legroom that comes with the added length.

Choose Split Top King If

You and your partner want more shoulder-to-shoulder space, a child or pet regularly ends up in the bed, or you would rather have the width and can spare a few extra inches of wall clearance.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much bigger is a Split Top King than a Split Top Cal King?

A King is 4 inches wider than a Cal King, and a Cal King is 4 inches longer than a King. The total sleeping surface is nearly identical between the two sizes.

Which size is better for tall sleepers?

Split Top Cal King is the better choice for anyone over roughly 6 feet 2 inches tall, since its 84-inch length gives extra legroom a standard King does not. Split Top King sleepers who are very tall may find their feet closer to the edge of the mattress.

Will a Split Top Cal King fit through a narrow doorway or staircase?

Its 72-inch width is narrower than a King's 76 inches, so a Cal King base and mattress generally clear tight doorways and staircases more easily. Always measure your specific doorway and stairwell before ordering either size.

Do both sizes offer the same split top features?

Yes, sizing only changes the mattress dimensions, not the split top functionality. Independent head, foot, and firmness control on each side works the same way in both Cal King and King.

Can I use California King sheets and bedding on a split top base?

Yes, standard California King sheets and bedding fit a split top Cal King mattress the same as they would a traditional one-piece Cal King mattress. King is the more widely available size for bedding, so shoppers who want the broadest linen selection often lean that way.
